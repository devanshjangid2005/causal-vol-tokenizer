## Slide 1 · Quantifying Look-Ahead Bias via Causal, Volatility-Aware Motif Tokenization  `[0:00 – 0:21]`

*On screen:* Title, team, and the context / origin / horizon motif

> Hello, we are presenting our DA4022 project on look-ahead bias inside motif tokenizers for financial time series. We built a leak-free volatility forecasting pipeline, then switched on one source of future information at a time and measured how much better each makes the forecasts look.

## Slide 2 · A tokenizer can see past the forecast origin in two ways  `[0:21 – 1:00]`

*On screen:* Timeline of context / origin t / horizon, with fit-time and encode-time leak markers

> A forecast at origin t may only use the context before t. A BPE motif tokenizer can break that in two ways. At fit time, a normalization window or a merge or decoding table can be estimated on data that overlaps the test period. At encode time, applying Zhang et al.'s definition of incremental tokenization, rank-ordered BPE is not incremental at an arbitrary origin, so a stream tokenized once and then split carries a token across t. Tokenizing only the context avoids that, but leaves a partial final token.

## Slide 3 · Measure each leak on its own, against a matched control  `[1:00 – 1:21]`

*On screen:* Research question and four contribution cards

> So our question is which parts of the pipeline leak, and by how much each one inflates measured accuracy. We contribute a channel-by-channel audit, a measurement of the boundary versus partial-token trade-off, a causal tokenizer design, and an evaluation with controls and a holdout scored once.

## Slide 4 · The causal reference pipeline, and where each leak switches on  `[1:21 – 1:56]`

*On screen:* Pipeline flowchart with channel markers 1–5

> Here is the causal reference pipeline. Five-minute returns are normalized by trailing EWMA volatility, quantized into 32 bins, and merged by BPE that never crosses a clock hour, with a scale token for each hour. A causal RoV times SEI gate, a mechanism borrowed from PATK, shortens tokens in volatile hours. A GPT with 855,169 parameters forecasts realized volatility with a direct head and a generative head. The orange markers show where each of the five leaks switches on.

## Slide 5 · One switch per arm, its own model, and two controls  `[1:56 – 2:31]`

*On screen:* Two-lane protocol diagram, inflation formula, negative and positive control cards

> Every leak arm differs from its matched control in exactly one switch, and each arm trains its own model, so the leak is present in training and in testing; only Channel 3 reuses the reference model. We compare their QLIKE losses on the same origins and report inflation: how much the leak flatters the forecast. A simulated GARCH negative control gives an oracle floor no causal model can beat, and a positive control shows an extreme leak for scale.

## Slide 6 · Six expanding walk-forward folds and one 2025 holdout  `[2:31 – 2:54]`

*On screen:* Gantt of 6 expanding folds + 2025 holdout with test-origin counts; statistics chips

> We use Binance BTCUSDT five-minute data from 2021 to 2025, in six expanding walk-forward folds with 26,290 test origins in total. 2025 is a holdout that was scored exactly once. Statistics include block bootstrap intervals, Diebold-Mariano tests with Holm correction, equivalence tests, random-effects pooling across folds and a model confidence set.

## Slide 7 · Pre-audit numbers stay withdrawn  `[2:54 – 3:31]`

*On screen:* Timeline 15 Oct → 21 Nov; table of withdrawn pre-audit figures and what replaced them

> Before the results, a note on continuity. Our first repository commit on 15 October held pre-audit numbers: a plus 16.6 percent Channel 4 leak, a GARCH baseline that looked 21.7 percent worse than our own model, and an 84 percent boundary mismatch. The audit that day traced them to bugs, and our midterm already withdrew them. Channel 1 rose from 7.09 to 63.39 percent because each arm now trains its own model against a matched control. Every number today comes from the corrected pipeline.

## Slide 8 · Hour boundaries make the tokenizer incremental at the origin  `[3:31 – 3:56]`

*On screen:* Native chart: straddle vs perturbation by tokenizer; callouts 0% / 57% / 1.40

> First, the token-level audit on Fold 1. With hour boundaries and the causal gate, no token straddles the origin and perturbing the future changes nothing. The centered gate of Channel 5 changes pre-origin tokens at 57 percent of origins, and unconstrained BPE straddles the origin at 32 percent. Motifs are short: 1.40 bars per token.

## Slide 9 · On simulated data, only the normalization leak beats the floor  `[3:56 – 4:24]`

*On screen:* Native chart: QLIKE by arm vs the 0.1268 oracle floor; Ch1 and positive control below

> Next, the negative control. On simulated GARCH data the oracle reaches a QLIKE of 0.1268. The reference and the other leak arms all stay above that floor. Only the Channel 1 normalization leak, at 0.0694, and the deliberate positive control fall below it. Channels 2, 4 and 5 still gain up to 2.12 percent over their controls there, but none crosses the floor.

## Slide 10 · Ch1 dominates: touching the horizon inflates accuracy by 63%  `[4:24 – 4:48]`

*On screen:* Native chart of 3-seed inflation per channel and head; callouts +63.39% and +62.03%

> Now the main result over six folds at the next-hour horizon, averaged over three seeds. Channel 1, where the normalization window reaches the horizon, cuts QLIKE by 63.39 percent on the direct head, with a random-effects estimate across folds of 62.03 percent and zero heterogeneity. Every other bar is within a few percent.

## Slide 11 · The other four channels are bounded below 3%  `[4:48 – 5:12]`

*On screen:* Native chart of 90% upper bounds for Ch2–Ch5; callouts +2.84% and +0.24%

> For the other channels we report how large they could be. The 90 percent upper bounds stay below 3 percent: the largest is Channel 5 on the direct head, at 2.84. Channel 5 direct and Channel 3 are significant after Holm correction, but tiny. Partial-token bias is not significant at this horizon.

## Slide 12 · A boundary leak can hide a large partial-token bias  `[5:12 – 5:47]`

*On screen:* Native chart: reference / context-only / stream-then-split QLIKE; callouts −19.53% and +16.10%

> This is the trade-off. At the next-day horizon, tokenizing only the context costs 19.53 percent, a partial-token bias. Tokenizing the stream and then splitting wins 16.10 percent back, landing at 0.3137 against a reference of 0.3128. The 2025 generative head shows the same pattern, while the 2025 direct head and the pooled folds show no such cost. So a leaky pipeline can look as good as a causal one. Both settings are single seed, so we call this an observed pattern.

## Slide 13 · At the next-day horizon every measured channel grows  `[5:47 – 6:10]`

*On screen:* Native chart of H = 288 inflation; scope chip; optimistic-p chip; positive control callout

> At the next-day horizon, on Fold 1, every measured channel grows: Channel 1 reaches 96.36 percent, Channel 4 16.10, Channel 5 7.02 and Channel 2 5.67. This is one fold, one seed and the direct head, and because hourly origins with 24-hour targets overlap, these p-values are likely optimistic.

## Slide 14 · 2025 holdout, scored once: Ch1 still inflates accuracy by about 70%  `[6:10 – 6:35]`

*On screen:* Native chart of holdout inflation; MCS chips (folds vs 2025)

> Then the 2025 holdout, scored once on 17 November and never re-scored. Channel 1 still inflates accuracy by 73.90 percent on the direct head and 68.32 on the generative head. Channel 2 flips sign across heads. In the model confidence set, leak-free GPT direct models lead on the folds, but on 2025 only GARCH remains.

## Slide 15 · Extensions: one future bar is already enough to leak  `[6:35 – 7:02]`

*On screen:* Three native charts (dose-response, cross-asset, healing); callouts +2.70% / +3.06% / +1.70%

> Finally, exploratory extensions on the folds only. One future bar in the normalization window already inflates accuracy by 2.70 percent, rising to 18.38 at six bars. Extra ETH data up to the split helps by 3.06 percent, while leaking ETH through the test period adds only 0.95, not significant after Holm. Token healing improves the same model by 1.70 percent.

> Now a short tour of the code. The repository runs from data through tokenization, models and experiments to results and this presentation. The leak tokenizer opens with a table pairing every leak arm with a control that differs in one switch. For Channel 1, the control takes the standard deviation over the context, and the leak stretches that window through the horizon. Channel 4 tokenizes past the origin, stops once the context is covered, and keeps the token that straddles the origin, so future bars ride inside it. The reference encoder works hour by hour, applying merges in rank order, so no token crosses an hour. The gate scores each hour on a trailing window; Channel 5 centers that window, so it peeks ahead. In the experiment runner, each arm trains its own model with the leak on in training and testing; only Channel 3 reuses the reference model, because decoding tables never enter training. Each leak faces its control in a Diebold-Mariano test with Holm correction. This guard refuses to re-score the 2025 holdout, and this block wrote the lock file; we show it, we never run it. Finally, live: the deck builder's check flag recomputes every quoted number from the results files and writes nothing. One mismatch stops the build.
